# Study design

## Study purpose

The study examines how people judge difficult candidate peaks when they receive progressively more algorithmic information.

## Conditions and fixed flow

1. **Signal only** — measured signal, smoothed signal, and candidate marker.
2. **Separate evidence** — the same signal plus separate clues about how clearly it stands out, repeatability after small signal changes, repeatability after setting changes, and shape warnings.
3. **AI says peak** — the same signal and evidence plus an explicit message that the AI marks the candidate as a peak and recommends accepting it.

The order is fixed for every participant.

## Shared cases

Three challenging cases are selected for each participant. The exact same signal is used in all three conditions. Case numbers stay aligned across conditions.

This provides a direct within-person comparison of whether the decision changes after evidence or AI advice is added. Repeating cases may also create learning, memory, or demand effects. Those effects should be treated as a limitation and checked through response time and confidence patterns.

## Case selection

The selector excludes obvious `clear_` cases and favors cases with one or more of the following:

- close to the detector cutoff;
- conflicting evidence;
- sensitivity to small signal changes;
- sensitivity to software settings;
- overlap or duplicate risk;
- disagreement between the hidden reference and algorithmic behavior.

## Primary outcomes

- Accept, Reject, or Defer.
- Confidence.
- Reference alignment.
- Decision change on the same case across conditions.
- Following or resisting the AI peak mark.
- Evidence use and reported confusion.

## End-of-study answers

The study comparison answer is hidden during all nine decisions. After completion, the participant sees a table containing the comparison label and their decisions in the three views.
